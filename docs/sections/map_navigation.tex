\subsection{Navigating with a Topological Map}
\label{sec:map_navigation}

\paragraph{Setup.}
We test whether a topological map of a town helps the image-goal policy reach
goals it cannot see from the start. The map is built once per town from a single
drive by a scripted explorer that covers every lane and every junction turn
(5.0\,km in Town02, 31.4\,km in Town03). Nodes are frames from this drive. We
compare three ways of choosing them: \emph{uniform} spacing along the drive,
\emph{entropy}, which keeps frames where the policy's action entropy peaks, and
\emph{SGM}~\cite{emmons2020sgm}, which merges frames that a learnt temporal
distance treats as interchangeable. The distance function follows
ViNT~\cite{shah2023vint} and is trained on the policy's demonstration data from
the training towns only. All three maps have the same number of key nodes
(255 in Town02, 1104 in Town03) and are completed with the same image-coverage
gap nodes and edge rules. Navigation is
image-only: the start and goal nodes are the best image matches of the current
view and the goal image, the agent is shown each subgoal's image along the
planned path, and a subgoal counts as reached when its image matches the current
view better than its neighbours. Episodes with no route are driven without the
map. We also report an upper bound that localises with the true pose.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Episodes come from the two held-out towns (Town02, Town03), with 100 per town in
each of three bands of road-route distance: easy (10--25\,m), medium
(25--50\,m) and hard (50--100\,m). These 600 episodes were drawn after the
method was fixed and were not used during development. Success requires
stopping within 1\,m of the goal, and leaving the road, crossing a solid line or
colliding ends the episode. All arms use the same policy and the same episodes,
so differences are tested with paired exact McNemar tests.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:map_navigation} gives the success rates. The map helps on long
routes: on the hard band all three maps raise success from 0.42 to 0.53--0.54
($p \le 0.002$ against no map). On easy and medium routes the gain is small and
not consistently significant, because the policy already reaches nearby goals
on its own. The choice of nodes makes no measurable difference: entropy and SGM
placement do not beat uniform spacing in any band. With ground-truth pose on maps of key nodes only
(Table~\ref{tab:map_navigation_pose}), uniform keeps its gain on hard routes
while entropy and SGM fall to the level of no map: without gap nodes their key
nodes leave long stretches of road uncovered. SPL is within
0.01 of SR everywhere: successful episodes drive at most 1.1 times the shortest
route, because a wrong turn almost always ends the episode by leaving the lane.
