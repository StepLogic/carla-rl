# v30 map arms on the long-distance benchmark (2026-11-02)

v30 step_300000, true goal image. The same 1,200 fixed episodes as the
benchmark in `v30_results_2026-11-02.md` §3: 4 held-out towns × 3 route bins
× 100 episodes. Success = within 1 m at ≤ 10 km/h; faults end the episode.

## Method

- **Maps.** One per town, built from the deterministic v30 exploration trace
  (`trace_v30det_<Town>`) with `scripts/build_image_maps.py` and
  `src/vae_nav/image_map.py`.
  - A node is a trace sample: its pose plus the frame the policy saw there.
  - Nodes are directional: they match only within a 60° heading.
- **Edges.** Only what the traced car drove: successive node visits (3 m,
  heading-aligned), weighted by metres driven, never across a jump restart.
- **Placements.** Uniform, random and SGM-matched have the same node count as
  real-time entropy.
  - Entropy uses the peer's online median/MAD spike rule (`online_maps.online_mad`): z = 1.0, 110 m window.
  - SGM uses a 10 m gate and cutoff 5, which merged almost nothing: the typical
    neighbour disagreement is about 50, so SGM ≈ a 4 m dense map.
  - SGM-matched bisects the cutoff to hit the entropy node count.
- **Navigation.** Localisation is by true pose.
  - The start node is the nearest aligned node within 8 m. The goal node is the
    reachable node within 8 m of the goal, minimising route length plus the
    remaining distance to the goal.
  - The subgoal is the next route node's frame, switching within 4 m (skipping
    ahead allowed). The GRU is kept across switches.
  - After the goal node, the true goal image is handed back.

## Why the maps do not help

1. **Routability is capped by the trace.** Even with a node every 2 m of trace,
   only 0.59 of the Town02/Town03 episodes (the two towns probed when this was
   measured) are routable at 8 m. The deterministic trace drove most roads in
   one direction only.
2. **The driver fails before a route ends.** Mapless, the policy faults about
   3 times per 100 m whatever the goal distance (2.8–3.6 between 10 and 100 m).
   That is a fault every ~30 m against routes of 10–100 m.
3. **Switching adds hazard.** On the same routable episodes, faults per 100 m
   rise with a map:

   | Placement | With map | Mapless |
   |---|---|---|
   | Uniform | 4.13 | 2.89 |
   | Random | 4.16 | 3.20 |
   | Entropy | 4.50 | 4.00 |

   - Of the uniform map's routable episodes, 468 of 634 die on a subgoal leg
     before the hand-back, which itself works (64 of 166 succeed after it).
   - Legs whose subgoal faces more than 45° off the route die at 0.55–0.64,
     but they are only ~3% of legs; aligned legs die at about 0.2 each.

**Conclusion.** A map can help only a driver whose mean distance to failure
exceeds the route length. On Gibson, DEBiT does not fail on contact, which is
why maps help there on long episodes (HM3D 15–35 m: +25 points; peer's result).

**Not tested.** Resetting the GRU at each switch (`EVAL_MAP_RESET_HIDDEN=1`).
It was the peer's standing rule, supported on small samples only.
